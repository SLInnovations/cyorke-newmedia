Three ways to talk someone round

Aristotle, about 2,300 years ago. Still what every ad and every essay runs on.

Logos

LOW-goss · the logic one

MEANS
Facts, numbers, experts. Proof someone could check.
YOURS
"The Government of Canada explains that algorithms push content people react to."

Pathos

PAY-thoss · the feelings one

MEANS
Makes the reader feel something.
YOURS
"Imagine a video of someone you know saying something they never said."

Ethos

EE-thoss · the trust one

MEANS
Why believe you? Name real sources.
YOURS
"MediaSmarts, a Canadian media literacy organisation, found that..."

Introductory paragraph

Hook, a bit of background, then your thesis as the very last sentence.

Hook

Introductory paragraph · step 1 of 3

WHAT THE OUTLINE SAYSTechnology is supposed to make life easier, but sometimes the things created to help people can create new problems that society is not ready for.

Your opening line. Say it so someone would keep reading.

Stuck? “What's the most annoying thing technology has done to you lately?” Start there.

Background / context

Introductory paragraph · step 2 of 3

WHAT THE OUTLINE SAYSIn the documentary Surviving Progress, a “progress trap” is defined as an advance that can cause harm when people fail to consider its consequences. Online information, social media and AI bring big benefits, but also risks that people, schools and governments are still learning to manage.

Explain what a progress trap is, in your words. You know this one cold.

Name the documentary. That's a source, and it counts.

Thesis (ONE sentence, 3 ideas)

Introductory paragraph · step 3 of 3

WHAT THE OUTLINE SAYSModern technology can become a progress trap because false information can spread quickly, it is hard to decide what information to trust, and it is developing faster than society can create rules to manage its risks.

One sentence. Your three reasons, in a row. This is the last line of the paragraph.

Keep all three in. Each one is a body paragraph, so they have to match what's coming.

Topic sentence

Body paragraph 3 · step 1 of 4

WHAT THE OUTLINE SAYSTechnology is developing so quickly that governments and schools are creating new rules and education programs to address risks that have already appeared.

Your words. What are governments and schools doing, and why now?

Stuck? Rules always turn up after the damage. Helmet laws after head injuries. Trail closures after the trails got wrecked.

Supporting point + source

Body paragraph 3 · step 2 of 4
