Progress trap

Something that makes life better at first, then causes a bigger problem later, because nobody thought it through.

SOMETHING IMPROVESE-bikes make the climb easy
→
EVERYONE DOES ITWay more riders on the trail
→
NEW PROBLEMTrails wreck, sections close

The trap isn't that e-bikes are bad. It's that nobody asked what happens after.

Claim and evidence

Two halves of the same job. A claim is what you want someone to believe. Evidence is why they should.

Claim

MEANS
What you want someone to believe. Somebody could disagree with it.
IS ONE
“Schools should ban AI homework tools.”
IS NOT ONE
“Our school banned AI homework tools in September.” That just happened. Nobody can argue with it.
Test it: could someone argue with this? Yes means claim.

Evidence

MEANS
The proof you give so people believe your claim. Facts, numbers, examples, or something an expert said.
IS ONE
“The Government of Canada says algorithms can spread false posts faster than true ones.”
IS NOT ONE
“Everyone knows AI is wrecking school.” Sounds like proof. No source, no number, no expert.
Test it: where did this come from, and could I check it?

Quick one before we start: “E-bikes have ruined the trails.” Claim or evidence?

Claim or evidence?

A claim is what you want someone to believe. Evidence is why they should.

Reasoning

The sentence that joins your evidence to your claim. It says why this proves that.

CLAIMTechnology spreads false information
→
EVIDENCEThe Government of Canada says algorithms amplify posts people react to
→
REASONINGFake stories get strong reactions, so the algorithm pushes them to more people

Without the last box, you've just dropped a fact on the reader and walked away.
